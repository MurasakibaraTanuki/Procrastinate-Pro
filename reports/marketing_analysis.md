# Marketing Analytics Report: Procrastinate Pro+

## Executive summary

Advertising did not recover its cost within the 14-day target for several important segments. The main pattern was not low customer value alone. High acquisition costs, particularly for US and iPhone users and for LambdaMediaAds traffic, outweighed comparatively strong LTV. The results support budget reallocation and controlled retention experiments, but they do not prove that any channel or customer attribute caused the losses.

## Results

### Device

iPhone and Mac users produced the highest 14-day LTV and conversion. However, iPhone acquisition did not reach ROI above 1 within the target period because CAC was also high. PC users showed comparatively strong paying-user retention, while retention declined across all devices.

### Region

US users generated the highest LTV, but their CAC was substantially higher than in other markets and 14-day ROI remained below 1. The UK showed the strongest combination of retention and ROI. Germany also presented a more favourable payback profile than the United States.

### Channel

LambdaMediaAds and RocketSuperAds generated high LTV. LambdaMediaAds also had exceptionally high CAC, which prevented satisfactory payback. FaceBoom and YRabbit were reported as ROI-positive, while TipTop and WahooNetBanner showed less stable performance. Organic traffic provides a useful benchmark but should not receive paid-media budget because it has no attributed acquisition spend in the dataset.

### Conversion and retention

Conversion was strongest among iPhone and Mac users. PC paying users retained relatively well, but non-paying retention was weak across segments. The broad decline after the first few days limited cumulative value.

## Interpretation

The descriptive evidence is consistent with an inefficient acquisition mix. The business attracted some high-value users at a price that exceeded their 14-day revenue contribution. Weak early retention further restricted the period available to recover acquisition costs.

The data do not isolate causal mechanisms. High CAC and low ROI may reflect campaign targeting, auction prices, attribution rules, customer mix or unobserved differences between cohorts. Segment comparisons should therefore guide experiments rather than automatic budget shifts.

## Recommendations

1. Reduce new spend on combinations that remain below the 14-day payback threshold, especially LambdaMediaAds campaigns aimed at US iPhone users.
2. Reallocate a limited test budget toward the UK, Germany and paid channels with consistently stronger cohort ROI. Keep control groups to measure incremental effects.
3. Audit campaign-level attribution before treating organic traffic or cross-device users as channel successes.
4. Test onboarding and early re-engagement interventions focused on the first three days. Evaluate incremental retention, conversion and 14-day ROI rather than engagement alone.
5. Review performance weekly by acquisition cohort. Use CAC, day-14 LTV and day-14 ROI together so that high revenue is not mistaken for profitability.

## Limitations

- The dataset is fictional and covers May to October 2019.
- The analysis is observational and descriptive.
- The report does not quantify uncertainty or control for campaign-level confounding.
- The 14-day horizon may understate value from customers with longer payback periods.
- Recommendations require validation through controlled budget and product experiments.
